# HerNext Network, copy deck

Every line of text on the site, organised by page and section, so wording can be
reviewed and revised here before it is applied to the HTML.

**Source.** Substance is drawn from the four HerNext Network correction
documents of 2026: the About page revision, and the full website corrections for
Our Work, Our Impact, and Partners and Collaboration. Those documents supply the
institutional language but no taglines, calls to action, form labels, error
messages or microcopy. Those were written for this site and are marked **[new]**
below.

Copy that predates the corrections and came from the Institutional Profile,
First Edition 2026, is marked **[profile]** where it survives unchanged.

**House style.**

- No em dashes. Use a comma, a colon, or the word *and*. The correction
  documents use em dashes and ampersands freely; both are re-typeset here.
- Ampersands are spelled out. *Enterprise and Livelihood Development*, not
  *Enterprise & Livelihood Development*.
- British spelling: organisation, recognise, programme, centred.
- One italic emphasis word per heading, no more.
- Sentence case for headings. Title case only for proper names of initiatives.
- Numerals `01` to `09` for sequences, spelled-out numbers in prose.

### Impact in action
- **Heading:** Starting with *evidence*
- **HerNext Kiambu Chapter.** HerNext's community-level approach begins by
  listening. Following the establishment of the chapter, women from different
  economic activities were engaged to understand their enterprises, livelihoods
  and most significant barriers to growth.
- Individual follow-up and baseline data collection were subsequently undertaken
  with women engaged in livestock enterprises, including dairy, poultry and pig
  farming. The assessment identified animal feed affordability and access as a
  major recurring challenge affecting participating livestock farmers.
- Rather than immediately prescribing a solution, HerNext is using these
  findings to inform the design of a targeted intervention and identify
  appropriate technical, commercial and development partners. The intervention
  will be designed to assess changes in production costs, productivity,
  profitability, market readiness and household livelihoods.
- **Callout:** This is the HerNext approach in practice: listen first,
  understand the evidence, intervene strategically and measure what changes.

**Kiambu is an example of the process, not proof of completed economic impact.
No outcome may be attributed to it until follow-up measurement is done.**

### From data to action
- **Heading:** What happens *next*
- Once a challenge has been identified, HerNext seeks partners capable of
  addressing different parts of the problem.
- In livestock, for example, collaboration may include animal nutrition and feed
  companies, veterinary and technical experts, financial institutions,
  agricultural research organisations, government and county institutions,
  development partners, processors and buyers.
- HerNext's role is to organise, connect, coordinate and measure, building an
  ecosystem around the economic challenge rather than attempting to solve every
  problem alone.

### How we measure impact
- **Heading:** Accountability through *evidence*
- **Lead:** Each HerNext programme establishes relevant baseline indicators

| Domain | Indicators |

| Agriculture | Production levels, input and feed costs, yield and productivity, mortality rates, farmer income. |
| Markets | Number of reliable buyers, sales volumes, value of contracts, new markets accessed. |
| Finance | Women accessing finance, value of finance accessed, investment readiness. |
| Trade | Businesses entering new markets, buyer connections, export opportunities converted into actual business. |

## Legal pages

Three pages written for this site, all **[new]**, all drafts pending review by
a qualified adviser. Each carries a visible note saying so, and each marks the
placeholders that must be filled in: `[registered entity name]`,
`[registered address]` and `[governing jurisdiction]`.

### Privacy policy *(privacy.html)*
Eleven sections: who we are, what we collect, why and on what basis, who else
handles it, where it goes, how long we keep it, your rights, security, children,
changes, governing law.

Written to describe what the site genuinely does rather than a generic template.
It names the three third parties that actually see visitor data, Formspree,
GitHub Pages and Google Fonts, states that the site sets no cookies of its own
and runs no analytics, and gives retention periods of twenty four months for
enquiries and thirty six for registrations. **If Google Fonts is ever
self-hosted, or analytics added, section 4 must change with it.**

Section 2 describes one form, not two. When the four-step application flow was
folded into the contact form, the field list narrowed to name, email,
organisation, subject and message. **If a longer intake form ever returns, that
list must grow with it.**

### Terms of use *(terms.html)*
Thirteen sections. Two carry most of the weight:

- **Section 2, the status of our programmes.** States in a boxed callout that
  the initiatives described are statements of intent, that registering is not an
  application to an open programme and creates no obligation, and that nothing
  on the site is an offer of employment, funding, investment, a grant or a
  scholarship.
- **Section 3, no fees and how to spot impersonation.** States that HerNext
  never charges to register or take part, names the only official channels
  (this website, info@hernextnetwork.com, hernextnetwork@gmail.com and Instagram
  @hernextnetworkltd), and gives an address to report anyone soliciting payment
  in its name.

### Accessibility statement *(accessibility.html)*
Commits to WCAG 2.1 AA, lists ten specific measures taken, and names four known
limitations honestly: the Google Fonts dependency, screen reader pairings not
yet exhaustively tested, photography still being replaced, and no independent
audit yet. Gives a contact route and a ten working day response aim.
